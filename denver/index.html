Complete Heating & Cooling Services for Denver Properties

We service every type of heating and cooling equipment you'll find in local homes and businesses, from central air conditioners and gas furnaces to heat pumps and ductless mini-splits. It doesn't matter whether the building is a single-family house, a townhome, or a busy office. Our technicians know the local climate, the age and construction of local homes, and the code requirements that apply to new installations, which pays off in every diagnosis.

Most homeowners we meet need one of three things: a repair, a replacement, or regular maintenance. Repairs are usually straightforward: a burned contactor, a dirty flame sensor, a refrigerant leak, or a seized blower motor. When we replace a system, it gets a proper load calculation, not a guess based on the old unit's tonnage, which keeps temperatures even and stops the new equipment from short-cycling its way to an early failure. Maintenance visits include coil cleaning, a refrigerant charge check, safety control testing, and a static pressure reading, and nothing does more to extend equipment life.

Commercial work is a big part of what we do too. We service rooftop units, split systems, and ventilation equipment for restaurants, retail stores, offices, and multi-unit properties, and we know that every hour of downtime hurts the business. To keep disruptions short, we carry common parts on every truck, diagnose quickly, and schedule repairs around your operating hours whenever possible.

Running it frozen risks an overflowing drain pan and a damaged compressor. A short-cycling furnace, or any burning smell, means it's time to kill power at the furnace switch and call us immediately.

Getting More Years From Your HVAC System in Denver

The best way to avoid a mid-season breakdown is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners replace filters every one to three months, keep vegetation trimmed two feet back from the outdoor condenser, rinse the condenser coil each spring, and schedule a professional tune-up twice a year, cooling in spring and heating in fall, so small problems get caught while they're still cheap.

How old is your system? Its age says a lot about what comes next. With regular service, most air conditioners reach 12 to 15 years, gas furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15. If your system is past that point and is also showing higher bills, uneven temperatures, or repeated repairs, it's usually due for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency replacement in peak season.

Small problems rarely stay small for long. Take a weak capacitor: replacing it is a short, inexpensive repair, but if it's ignored for a season, it can burn out the compressor, and just like that a minor fix has become a full system replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to keep our number in their phone and call at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves money and spares you a miserable night.
